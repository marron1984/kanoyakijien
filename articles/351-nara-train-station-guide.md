# Nara Train Station Guide: Navigating Kintetsu and JR Stations

Nara is unusual among Japanese cities in that it has two entirely separate main train stations, operated by two different railway companies, located roughly ten minutes' walk apart. For first-time visitors, this can create a moment of confusion — which station should you arrive at, which should you depart from, and what will you find when you get there?

The answer, as with most things in Nara, is simpler than it initially appears. Both stations are well-maintained, clearly signed, and connected to the city's main attractions. But they serve different purposes and offer different advantages, and understanding the distinction before you arrive will save you time and unnecessary walking.

## The Two-Station System

Nara's dual-station setup reflects the broader structure of Japanese rail. Kintetsu (formally the Kintetsu Railway Company) and JR West (Japan Railways West) are separate private and semi-private companies operating independent networks. They share no ticketing, no platforms, and no buildings in Nara. You cannot walk between them underground or use a single ticket on both.

This is not a design flaw — it is simply how Japanese railways evolved. For travellers, the practical consequence is that your choice of station depends on where you are coming from, what ticket you hold, and where in Nara you want to be.

## Kintetsu Nara Station

### Location and Layout

Kintetsu Nara is the more centrally located of the two stations. It sits underground, beneath a modest building on Noborioji-dori, at the eastern edge of Nara's commercial district. The station opened in its current underground configuration in 1969, replacing an earlier surface station.

The concourse is on the basement level, with two island platforms serving four tracks. Trains arrive from and depart toward Kyoto (to the north) and Osaka-Namba (to the west). The station is compact and easy to navigate — there is essentially one main corridor with ticket gates at one end and platforms at the other.

### Exits and Orientation

Kintetsu Nara has multiple exits, and choosing the right one saves time:

- **Exit 1 (East exit)**: Leads directly toward Nara Park and Kofukuji Temple. This is the exit for most sightseeing purposes. Within two minutes of emerging, you can see deer on the grass.
- **Exit 2 (Higashimuki exit)**: Opens onto the Higashimuki covered shopping street, useful for restaurants, cafes, and shops. Also a good exit for Naramachi, which begins just south of the shopping arcades.
- **Exit 3–5 (West and south exits)**: Lead to the bus terminal and the western side of the city. Less commonly used by tourists but necessary for certain bus routes.

A large bronze statue of Gyoki, the revered Buddhist monk, stands in the small plaza above exit 2. This is Nara's most common meeting point — if someone tells you to meet at the station, this is where they mean.

### Facilities

Kintetsu Nara Station is well-equipped for travellers:

- **Coin lockers**: Available in various sizes on the concourse level. Small lockers (around 400 yen) fit daypacks, medium lockers (around 600 yen) fit carry-on luggage, and large lockers (around 800 yen) accommodate full-size suitcases. These fill up by mid-morning during peak seasons, so arrive early or use the luggage storage counter nearby.
- **Tourist information centre**: Located at exit 1, staffed by English-speaking attendants who provide maps, bus schedules, and event information. Open daily from 9:00 to 21:00.
- **Convenience stores**: A Family Mart operates on the concourse level, useful for water, snacks, and cash withdrawal from the ATM.
- **Restrooms**: Clean, well-maintained facilities on the concourse level. Western-style and Japanese-style toilets are both available.
- **Wi-Fi**: Free Kintetsu Wi-Fi is available on the concourse. Connection is straightforward, though speeds vary.

### Connections from Kintetsu Nara

Kintetsu Nara connects directly to:

- **Kyoto** (Kintetsu Kyoto Station): Limited Express takes approximately 35 minutes (around 1,280 yen with surcharge). Ordinary and Express trains take 45–50 minutes (around 760 yen). Trains depart every 10–30 minutes depending on type.
- **Osaka-Namba**: Express takes roughly 40 minutes (around 680 yen). Trains are frequent throughout the day.
- **Yamato-Saidaiji**: A major transfer hub 5 minutes west, where you can connect to lines toward Nishi-Nara, Tenri, or Kashihara.

The Kintetsu network is generally faster and more convenient for reaching Nara's city centre from Kyoto and central Osaka.

## JR Nara Station

### Location and Layout

JR Nara Station sits above ground on the western side of the city, roughly 800 metres from Kintetsu Nara. The station building was renovated in the early 2000s and has a clean, functional design. An older, elegant Meiji-era station building stands adjacent — now used as a tourist information and community space — and is worth a glance.

The station has two main platforms serving multiple tracks. The concourse is at ground level, with ticket gates opening onto a covered bus terminal to the east and a quieter residential area to the west.

### Exits and Orientation

- **East exit**: Faces toward the city centre and Nara Park. From here, walk straight along Sanjō-dori for about ten minutes to reach the Kintetsu Nara area and the edge of the park.
- **West exit**: Opens onto a residential neighbourhood with fewer tourist facilities. Some budget hotels are in this direction.

### Facilities

JR Nara Station offers:

- **Coin lockers**: Available in the same size range as Kintetsu, located near the ticket gates. Availability is generally better here than at Kintetsu Nara.
- **Tourist information**: The old station building houses a tourist information desk with English-speaking staff.
- **Vierra Nara**: A small commercial complex attached to the station, with a bakery, restaurants, and a supermarket.
- **Convenience stores**: Available both inside the ticket gate area and outside.
- **Restrooms and Wi-Fi**: Standard JR West facilities. Free JR West Wi-Fi is available.

### Connections from JR Nara

JR Nara connects to:

- **Kyoto** (JR Kyoto Station): The Miyakoji Rapid takes approximately 45 minutes (around 730 yen). Ordinary trains take longer, roughly 70 minutes. Trains depart every 15–30 minutes.
- **Osaka** (JR Tennoji/Osaka Station): The Yamatoji Rapid takes approximately 30–50 minutes depending on your destination station.
- **Horyuji**: A local JR line reaches Horyuji Station in about 12 minutes, useful for visiting Horyu-ji Temple, one of the world's oldest wooden structures.

JR Nara is the obvious choice for Japan Rail Pass holders, as all JR services are covered by the pass.

## Which Station Should You Use?

The decision depends on three factors:

**Where you are coming from**: If arriving from Kyoto's central station or Osaka-Namba, Kintetsu is faster and more convenient. If arriving from Osaka's Tennoji area, Shin-Osaka, or anywhere on the JR network, JR Nara makes more sense.

**What pass you hold**: Japan Rail Pass holders should use JR lines, as Kintetsu trains are not covered. If you hold a Kintetsu Rail Pass or Kansai Thru Pass, Kintetsu services are included.

**Where you are staying**: Most centrally located accommodation — including traditional ryokan in the Naramachi district — is within comfortable walking distance of Kintetsu Nara Station. Guests staying at Kanoya, for example, find the walk from Kintetsu Nara a pleasant introduction to the city, passing through quiet streets that transition from commercial bustle to the calm of the historic quarter.

## Walking Between the Two Stations

The walk between Kintetsu Nara and JR Nara takes 10–12 minutes along Sanjō-dori, a flat, straight shopping street. The route is entirely on pavement, well-lit, and easy to follow. In good weather it is a pleasant stroll; in rain or with heavy luggage, you may prefer to take the bus (several routes connect the two stations for around 220 yen) or a taxi (roughly 800 yen).

## Tips for Using Nara's Stations

- **IC cards**: Both stations accept Suica, Pasmo, ICOCA, and other major IC cards. If you have one, tap-and-go is the easiest payment method.
- **Last trains**: The final departures to Kyoto and Osaka are typically between 22:30 and 23:15. Verify the exact times on your travel day, as schedules can shift seasonally.
- **Morning arrivals**: If you are visiting Nara as a day trip, arriving before 9:00 gives you quieter temples and better locker availability.
- **Luggage forwarding**: If you are travelling with large bags, consider using a takkyubin luggage forwarding service from your previous hotel. Both stations also have temporary luggage storage if lockers are full.
- **Station numbering**: Kintetsu Nara is station A28 on the Kintetsu Nara Line. JR Nara does not use a numbered system in the same way but is clearly marked on all JR West maps.

## Nearby Stations Worth Knowing

A few other stations in the Nara area are useful for specific destinations:

- **Kintetsu Nara Park Station (proposed)**: Not yet operational. Ignore outdated references.
- **Shin-Omiya Station (Kintetsu)**: One stop west of Kintetsu Nara. Useful if staying in the Shin-Omiya hotel district.
- **Kasuga Taisha-mae (bus stop)**: Not a train station, but the main bus stop for Kasuga Grand Shrine, reachable by bus from either station.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Nara station is closest to Nara Park?

Kintetsu Nara Station is closer. Exit 1 puts you within a two-minute walk of the park's western edge and Kofukuji Temple's grounds. JR Nara Station requires a 15–20 minute walk to reach the same area.

### Can I use a Japan Rail Pass on Kintetsu trains?

No. The Japan Rail Pass covers only JR lines. Kintetsu is a separate private railway. JR Pass holders should use JR Nara Station.

### Are there coin lockers at Nara stations?

Yes, both stations have coin lockers in small (around 400 yen), medium (around 600 yen), and large (around 800 yen) sizes. During peak seasons, arrive early for the best availability.

### How far apart are Kintetsu Nara and JR Nara stations?

Approximately 800 metres, or a 10–12 minute walk along the flat, straight Sanjō-dori shopping street.

### What is the last train from Nara to Kyoto?

The last Kintetsu Express typically departs around 23:15, and the last JR Miyakoji Rapid around 22:30. Always verify current schedules, as times can vary seasonally.
