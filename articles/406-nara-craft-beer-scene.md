# Nara's Craft Beer Scene: Microbreweries and Taprooms

Japan's craft beer revolution is well documented in Tokyo, Osaka, and Yokohama. What is less widely known is that Nara — a city most visitors associate with temples, deer, and sake — has developed a craft beer scene of genuine quality and character. It is small, as befits a city of 350,000 people, but what it lacks in scale it compensates for in personality, local identity, and the willingness of its brewers to experiment with ingredients and styles rooted in the region.

For visitors who have spent a day walking temple grounds and navigating crowds, a well-made craft beer in a quiet taproom offers a welcome change of pace and a connection to a different side of modern Japanese culture. Nara's brewers are part of a national movement that has seen the number of Japanese microbreweries grow from a handful in the mid-1990s to over 600 today, but they bring something distinctly local to their work.

## A Brief History of Craft Beer in Japan

Japan's beer culture was historically dominated by four major companies: Asahi, Kirin, Sapporo, and Suntory. Until 1994, Japanese law required beer producers to brew a minimum of 2,000 kilolitres per year — a threshold that effectively prevented small-scale brewing. The revision of this law in 1994, reducing the minimum to 60 kilolitres, opened the door to microbreweries.

The first wave of Japanese craft beer, often called ji-biru (local beer), was uneven in quality. Many early producers were tourism-oriented ventures with more enthusiasm than expertise. The second wave, from roughly 2010 onward, brought serious brewing talent, international training, and higher standards. Nara's current crop of brewers belongs firmly to this second wave — they are technically skilled, stylistically adventurous, and deeply connected to local ingredients and identity.

## Nara's Breweries and Taprooms

### Naramachi Brewery

Situated in the heart of Naramachi, this small-batch operation produces ales that reflect both Western brewing traditions and Japanese sensibility. The taproom occupies a renovated machiya townhouse, and the contrast between the traditional wooden architecture and the stainless steel taps is characteristic of Nara's approach to modernity — not replacing the old but fitting the new within it.

Expect a rotating selection of four to six beers on tap, typically including a pale ale, an IPA, a seasonal offering, and something experimental. The brewers have developed a reputation for using local Nara ingredients — yuzu citrus, Yamato-cha green tea, and locally grown hops — in beers that remain balanced and drinkable rather than gimmicky.

The taproom seats approximately 20 people, and the atmosphere is convivial without being rowdy. Food is limited to bar snacks, but the brewers are happy to recommend nearby restaurants.

### Nara Brewing Company

A larger operation with wider distribution, Nara Brewing Company produces beers available in bottles and cans at local shops as well as on tap at several bars and restaurants in the city. Their core range includes a clean, crisp lager, a citrus-forward wheat beer, and a robust porter that pairs remarkably well with the rich flavours of Nara's traditional cuisine.

Their seasonal releases are worth seeking out. Autumn brings a kabocha (pumpkin) ale that has become a local favourite, and spring sees a cherry blossom pale ale that uses actual sakura petals in the brewing process — subtle rather than sweet, with a delicate floral note that disappears before it becomes cloying.

### Golden Rabbit Beer

One of Nara Prefecture's most distinctive craft beer brands, Golden Rabbit (named after the rabbit motif associated with various Nara shrines) operates with a focus on Belgian-inspired styles adapted to Japanese tastes. Their saison is exceptional — dry, peppery, and refreshingly tart, with a complexity that rewards slow drinking.

Golden Rabbit's beers are available at their taproom and at selected restaurants and bars in the Nara area. The taproom itself is worth a visit for the brewer's knowledge alone — conversations about ingredients, techniques, and the philosophy of brewing are welcomed.

### Beer Bars and Multi-Tap Venues

For those who want to sample broadly rather than committing to a single brewery, Nara has several bars that stock a rotating selection of craft beers from across Japan and the local region.

**Craft Beer Nara**: A dedicated craft beer bar near Kintetsu Nara Station with 10 to 15 taps, featuring a mix of local Nara breweries and guest beers from across Japan. The staff are knowledgeable and happy to guide you through the selection.

**Standing bars in Higashimuki**: The covered shopping arcade of Higashimuki and its side streets contain several standing bars (tachinomi) that have added craft beer taps alongside their traditional sake and shochu offerings. These are casual, inexpensive, and social — a good way to sample a beer or two before dinner.

## Pairing Craft Beer with Nara Cuisine

One of the pleasures of Nara's craft beer scene is its compatibility with the city's food culture. While sake is the traditional pairing for Japanese cuisine, well-made craft beer can be equally effective:

- **IPA with grilled yakitori**: The hop bitterness cuts through the richness of charcoal-grilled chicken and its sweet tare sauce.
- **Wheat beer with tempura**: A light, citrusy wheat beer complements the delicate crispness of vegetable and shrimp tempura without overwhelming it.
- **Porter or stout with nara-zuke pickles**: The rich, roasted malt flavours of dark beers harmonise with the deep, savoury-sweet profile of Nara's signature sake-lees pickles.
- **Saison with kaiseki**: A dry, complex saison can accompany the progression of a multi-course kaiseki meal in much the same way a dry white wine might — cleansing the palate between dishes without competing for attention.

Several restaurants in Nara now offer craft beer alongside their sake selections, recognising that the two traditions are complementary rather than competitive. Ask your server for pairing recommendations — the willingness to match beer with Japanese food is a sign of a restaurant that takes both seriously.

## The Sake Connection

Nara's craft beer scene exists in the shadow of a far older brewing tradition. Nara is widely considered the birthplace of modern sake — the pasteurisation technique and the polishing methods that define contemporary sake production were developed at temples in the Nara region during the medieval period. The city's sake breweries are centuries old.

This heritage influences rather than inhibits the craft beer scene. Several of Nara's craft brewers have backgrounds in sake production, and the precision, attention to water quality, and respect for fermentation that characterise sake brewing carry over into their beer-making. Nara's water — soft, clean, and drawn from mountain aquifers — is excellent for brewing both sake and beer.

Visitors interested in both traditions can easily combine sake brewery visits and craft beer taproom sessions within a single day or evening. The two experiences illuminate different facets of Nara's relationship with fermentation and flavour.

## Practical Information

### When to Go

Taprooms typically open in the late afternoon — 16:00 or 17:00 — and close by 22:00 or 23:00. Some have limited daytime hours on weekends. Weekday evenings are the least crowded. Friday and Saturday evenings at popular spots can mean waiting for a seat.

### Cost

Craft beer in Nara typically costs between 600 and 900 yen per glass (approximately 300 to 400 ml), slightly higher than mass-market alternatives but consistent with craft beer pricing throughout Japan. Most taprooms accept cash and credit cards, though some smaller venues remain cash-only.

### Combining with an Evening in Nara

For those staying overnight — and particularly for guests at a ryokan like Kanoya, where the evening kaiseki dinner is the culinary centrepiece — a pre-dinner craft beer in Naramachi provides a pleasant way to transition from the day's sightseeing to the evening's rest. Walk the lantern-lit streets, stop for a beer, then return for dinner and the onsen. It is a rhythm that suits Nara's pace.

### Language

Most taproom staff have enough English for beer ordering, and many have tasting notes in English. The universal language of pointing at the tap list also works perfectly well.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is craft beer popular in Nara?

Craft beer is growing in popularity in Nara, as it is across Japan. The scene is small compared to Tokyo or Osaka but is well-established, with several dedicated breweries and bars. Younger Japanese visitors and international travellers are the primary audience.

### Can I buy Nara craft beer to take home?

Yes. Several Nara breweries sell bottles and cans at their taprooms and at local shops. Check airline regulations for carrying liquids in luggage. Some breweries offer shipping within Japan.

### Are taprooms suitable for non-drinkers?

Most taprooms offer non-alcoholic options including soft drinks and sometimes non-alcoholic beer. The atmosphere is relaxed and inclusive.

### Where is the best area for craft beer in Nara?

Naramachi and the streets near Kintetsu Nara Station offer the highest concentration of craft beer options within walking distance of each other.

### Can I visit a brewery for a tour?

Some breweries offer informal tours by arrangement — contact them in advance. Larger operations like Nara Brewing Company are more likely to accommodate visitors than very small taproom-breweries with limited space.
