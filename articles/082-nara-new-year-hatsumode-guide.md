# New Year in Nara: Hatsumode and Winter Celebrations

New Year (Shogatsu) is Japan's most important holiday — a period of renewal, reflection, and the first shrine visit of the year that sets the spiritual tone for the months ahead. In Nara, New Year carries a particular weight. When you make your first shrine visit of the year at a place that has been sacred for over 1,200 years — walking paths worn by centuries of similar pilgrimages, surrounded by an ancient forest, approaching a shrine that has greeted worshippers since 768 CE — the act acquires a depth that more recently established shrines cannot provide.

Nara at New Year is quiet, cold, and beautiful. The city has an austere winter clarity — bare trees revealing architectural details that foliage conceals, frost on stone lanterns, deer in their thick winter coats. For travellers who can embrace the cold, New Year in Nara offers one of the most authentic and atmospheric cultural experiences available in Japan.

## Hatsumode: The First Shrine Visit

### What It Is

Hatsumode is the tradition of visiting a shrine or temple during the first days of the new year — ideally on January 1, but the custom extends through January 3 (and informally through the first week). The visit involves prayer, the purchase of new amulets and charms (replacing the previous year's), and drawing an omikuji (fortune slip) to forecast the year ahead.

### Kasuga Taisha

Kasuga Taisha is Nara's principal hatsumode destination. The shrine, founded in 768 CE and dedicated to the deity who arrived on a white deer, draws tens of thousands of visitors during the New Year period.

**New Year's Eve (December 31)**:
- The shrine grounds are prepared with special decorations
- From approximately 11:00pm, worshippers begin gathering for the midnight transition
- At midnight, the new year is welcomed with prayer and the shrine's first ceremony

**January 1–3**:
- The shrine is open for hatsumode throughout the day
- Expect significant crowds, particularly on January 1 morning
- The approach through the lantern-lined forest path is atmospheric even in (or especially in) the crowds
- Special amulets and ema (votive plaques) for the new year are available

### Todai-ji

Todai-ji offers its own New Year tradition: the front windows of the Great Buddha Hall are opened on the evening of December 31 and the morning of January 1, allowing the face of the Great Buddha to be seen from outside. The image of the Buddha's serene face illuminated against the night, visible through the hall's great facade, is one of Nara's most powerful seasonal experiences.

### Other Temples

**Kofuku-ji**: Accessible for New Year visits with a view of the illuminated five-storey pagoda.

**Gangō-ji**: A quieter alternative for hatsumode in the heart of Naramachi.

**Shin-Yakushi-ji**: For those seeking a contemplative rather than crowded New Year experience.

## The Wakakusa Yamayaki

### The Mountain Burning

On the fourth Saturday of January, the dead grass on Mount Wakakusa — the gentle slope visible behind Todai-ji — is set ablaze in a spectacular fire ceremony. The entire hillside burns in a controlled conflagration that lights the night sky and is visible from across Nara.

The ceremony begins in the early evening:

1. **5:30pm**: A procession from Kasuga Taisha to the base of the mountain
2. **6:15pm**: Fireworks display
3. **6:30pm**: The grass is ignited. The fire spreads rapidly up the 342-metre hill, creating a wall of flame that burns for approximately 30 minutes.

The origins of the ceremony are debated — variously attributed to boundary disputes between temples, to pest control, or to a purification ritual. Whatever its origin, the visual effect is unforgettable: an entire mountain ablaze behind the ancient temples of Nara.

**Viewing**: The best views are from Nara Park (the Tobihino meadow area), the rooftop terraces of some buildings in the station area, and the Heijo Palace site. Arrive early — viewing areas fill well before the ceremony begins.

## Winter Atmosphere

### The City

Nara in winter has a clarity that other seasons lack. The air is crisp, the sky often blue, and the absence of foliage reveals the architecture — roof lines, column proportions, the shapes of temple buildings — with a precision that warmer seasons soften. The deer, in their thick winter coats, are at their most photogenic. Morning mist in the park is more frequent and more dramatic.

### Shika-yose (Deer Calling)

On selected winter mornings (typically December through March), the Nara Deer Preservation Foundation sounds a traditional horn call that summons deer from across the park to a central meadow for supplementary feeding. The sight of hundreds of deer streaming across frosty grass in response to the horn — an event that echoes a tradition from the Edo period — is unique to Nara and genuinely remarkable.

**Time**: Typically 10:00am on designated days. Check the schedule (available from the tourist information centre).

### Winter Light

The low winter sun creates dramatic conditions for photography and simply for seeing. Morning light rakes across the Great Buddha Hall's facade. Afternoon sun ignites the golden details of Kasuga Taisha. The early sunset (around 5:00pm in January) means blue hour begins mid-afternoon, and the illuminated pagoda at Kofuku-ji is visible against a still-blue sky.

## Practical Considerations

### Weather

January in Nara is cold but not extreme: daytime temperatures 3–8°C, overnight temperatures 0–3°C (occasionally below freezing). Snow is rare but possible. Rain is infrequent. Clear, cold days are typical.

**Dress**: Thermal base layers, warm jacket, hat, gloves, scarf. Layering is essential — temple interiors can be cold (they are unheated), but walking generates warmth.

### Accommodation

New Year accommodation in Nara should be booked well in advance — the holiday period (December 28–January 4) is peak domestic travel time. Some ryokan offer special New Year packages including traditional osechi cuisine (New Year's food served in tiered lacquer boxes).

Properties in Naramachi like Kanoya are ideally located for walking to both Kasuga Taisha and Todai-ji for New Year ceremonies, eliminating the need for transport on crowded nights.

### Transport

Train services run on holiday schedules during the New Year period. Some services are reduced; others are extended for late-night New Year's Eve travel. Check Kintetsu and JR schedules in advance.

### Food

Many restaurants close for the New Year period (December 31–January 2 or 3). Confirm dining plans with your accommodation. Ryokan with included meals provide the most reliable New Year dining. Convenience stores remain open throughout.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Nara crowded at New Year?

Kasuga Taisha is very busy on January 1. Other sites are less crowded. By January 2–3, crowds diminish significantly. The rest of the city remains quiet.

### Can I attend the midnight ceremony at Kasuga Taisha?

Yes. Arrive by 11:00pm on December 31 for the best position. Dress very warmly — you will be standing outside in near-freezing temperatures.

### When is the Wakakusa Yamayaki?

The fourth Saturday of January. The fire begins around 6:30pm. Arrive at your viewing position by 5:00pm.

### Is New Year a good time to visit Nara?

Yes — the winter atmosphere, hatsumode traditions, and Yamayaki ceremony create a unique and memorable experience. Book accommodation early and prepare for cold weather.
