# Nara Emergency Guide: Hospitals, Police, and What to Do in a Crisis

Nobody plans for emergencies while imagining their trip to Nara. You picture feeding deer in the park, admiring the Great Buddha at Todai-ji, and walking the lantern-lined paths of Kasuga Taisha — not searching for a hospital at midnight or explaining a lost passport to police. But knowing what to do in a crisis before one happens is the difference between a manageable inconvenience and a genuinely distressing experience.

Nara is one of the safest cities in one of the safest countries in the world. Violent crime against tourists is virtually unheard of, and the city's infrastructure is well-maintained. But medical emergencies, natural disasters, and accidents do not respect geography, and having the right information at hand — phone numbers, hospital locations, embassy contacts — can matter enormously when something goes wrong.

## Emergency Phone Numbers

Memorise or save these numbers before you arrive:

- **110**: Police (keisatsu). For crimes, accidents, or situations requiring police response. Operators may speak limited English.
- **119**: Fire and ambulance (shoubou/kyuukyuu). For medical emergencies, fires, and rescue. Specify whether you need an ambulance (kyuukyuusha) or fire service (shoubousha).
- **0570-000-911**: Japan Helpline. A 24-hour, English-language emergency assistance line for foreign visitors. This is invaluable when language barriers complicate a situation.
- **03-3580-3311**: British Embassy Tokyo (for UK citizens). Other embassies maintain similar emergency lines.
- **075-315-5970**: US Consulate Osaka-Kobe (for American citizens).

When calling 110 or 119, the operator will ask for your location. In Japan, addresses can be confusing. If possible, provide the name of a nearby landmark, convenience store, or the address displayed on a telephone pole or building. In Nara's main tourist areas, saying "near Todai-ji" or "Kintetsu Nara Station" will help dispatchers orient quickly.

## Hospitals and Medical Facilities

### For Emergencies

**Nara Medical University Hospital** (Nara Kenritsu Ika Daigaku Fuzoku Byouin)
- Location: Kashihara (approximately 40 minutes south of central Nara by train)
- This is the region's most advanced medical facility with a full emergency department. While not centrally located, it handles complex emergencies and has some English-speaking staff.

**Nara Prefectural General Medical Centre** (Nara Kenritsu Sogo Iryo Centre)
- Location: Central Nara, near Kintetsu Shin-Omiya Station
- The most accessible major hospital for visitors staying in central Nara. Has an emergency department and is reachable by taxi from the main tourist areas in approximately 10 minutes.

**Nara City Hospital** (Nara Shimin Byouin)
- Location: Eastern Nara
- A general hospital with emergency services. Smaller than the prefectural centre but closer to Nara Park.

### For Non-Emergency Medical Needs

**Nara Tourist Medical Support** — The Nara Visitors Bureau maintains a list of clinics with English-speaking doctors or translation services. Ask at the tourist information centres at either Kintetsu Nara or JR Nara stations for the most current list.

For minor ailments — headaches, stomach upset, mild allergic reactions — Japanese pharmacies (yakkyoku) are well-stocked. Look for chains like Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Sugi Yakkyoku, or Cocokara Fine. Pharmacists can recommend over-the-counter medications, though dosages in Japan tend to be lower than Western equivalents. If you need specific medications, bring them from home with appropriate documentation.

### What to Expect at a Japanese Hospital

Japanese hospitals are clean, professional, and efficient. Foreign visitors should be prepared for:

- **Payment at time of service**: Hospitals typically expect payment before discharge. Bring a credit card, as not all facilities accept international cards at all hours. Cash is always accepted.
- **Paperwork**: You will be asked to fill out forms, usually in Japanese. Staff will assist, and many hospitals in Nara have forms available in English.
- **Waiting times**: Emergency departments prioritise by severity, not arrival order. If your condition is not life-threatening, be prepared to wait.
- **Translation**: If you cannot communicate effectively, call the Japan Helpline (0570-000-911) or ask hospital staff to access the telephone interpretation service that many medical facilities subscribe to.

Keep all receipts and medical documentation for insurance claims.

## Police and Security

### Nara Police Station

The main Nara Police Station (Nara Keisatsu-sho) is located in central Nara, south of Sanjō-dori. For non-emergency matters — filing a report for lost property, a theft report for insurance purposes, or seeking directions — this is where to go. Staff are helpful and accustomed to dealing with foreign visitors, though English proficiency varies.

### Koban (Police Boxes)

Japan's koban system places small police boxes throughout urban areas. In Nara, you will find koban near both train stations, near Nara Park, and at major intersections. Koban officers can help with:

- Directions
- Lost property
- Minor incidents
- Translation assistance (some koban have access to telephone interpretation)

If you are lost or confused, a koban is always a safe place to ask for help. Officers are uniformed and approachable.

### Filing a Police Report

If you are a victim of theft or need a police report for insurance purposes, go to the nearest koban or the main police station. You will be asked to provide:

- Your passport
- A description of what happened
- Details of lost or stolen items
- Your local address (your hotel or accommodation address)

The police will issue a report (higai todoke), which you will need for insurance claims. This process can take 30 minutes to an hour. Japanese police are thorough and patient; the process may feel slow, but it is handled with care.

## Natural Disasters

### Earthquakes

Japan experiences thousands of earthquakes annually. Most are minor and barely noticeable, but larger quakes occur periodically. If you feel a strong earthquake:

1. **Drop, cover, and hold on.** Get under a sturdy table or desk. Protect your head and neck.
2. **Stay indoors if you are inside.** Do not run outside during shaking — falling debris is the primary danger.
3. **If outdoors**, move away from buildings, power lines, and anything that could fall. Open areas like Nara Park are relatively safe.
4. **After shaking stops**, check for injuries and hazards. Be prepared for aftershocks.
5. **Check your phone.** Japan's earthquake early warning system sends alerts to mobile phones. If you have a Japanese SIM or an international phone with the correct settings, you may receive these warnings seconds before shaking begins.

Japanese buildings, including hotels and ryokan, are built to stringent earthquake codes. Modern structures in Nara are designed to withstand significant seismic events.

### Typhoons

Typhoon season runs from approximately June through October. Nara is inland and generally experiences less severe effects than coastal areas, but strong winds and heavy rain can still cause disruptions. If a typhoon is approaching:

- Monitor NHK World (English-language news) and the Japan Meteorological Agency website.
- Follow instructions from your accommodation. Staff at properties like Kanoya are experienced in guiding guests through weather events and will advise on safety precautions and any necessary itinerary adjustments.
- Stay indoors during the storm. Avoid rivers and hillsides.
- Expect train services to be suspended or reduced. Services usually resume within hours of the typhoon passing.

### Flooding and Landslides

Heavy rains can cause localised flooding and landslides, particularly in hilly areas south and east of the city. Stay on established paths, avoid rivers during heavy rain, and heed any warnings from local authorities.

## Lost Passport

Losing your passport in Japan is stressful but manageable:

1. **File a police report** at the nearest koban or police station.
2. **Contact your embassy or consulate.** Most embassies can issue emergency travel documents within one to two business days. For many Western countries, the nearest consulate is in Osaka, approximately one hour from Nara.
3. **Visit the local immigration office** if needed for any visa-related issues.
4. **Keep photocopies** of your passport's data page and visa, stored separately from the original. A digital copy in your email or cloud storage is also wise.

## Useful Apps and Resources

- **NHK World** (app/website): English-language news with real-time disaster coverage.
- **Google Maps**: Useful for locating the nearest hospital, police box, or pharmacy. Works well in Nara.
- **Google Translate**: The camera function can translate Japanese signs and documents in real time. Download the Japanese language pack for offline use.

## Preparing Before You Go

A few minutes of preparation can make an enormous difference:

- **Save emergency numbers** in your phone. Add them to your contacts before departure.
- **Carry your accommodation's address and phone number** in written form. If you become disoriented or need a taxi, showing a driver a card with your hotel's address (ideally in Japanese) is the fastest way home.
- **Bring sufficient medication** for your entire trip, plus extra in case of delays. Carry prescriptions in their original packaging with a doctor's note.
- **Register with your embassy's travel notification programme** if one exists (e.g., the US State Department's STEP programme or the UK's FCDO registration).
- **Share your itinerary** with someone at home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the emergency number in Japan?

Call 110 for police and 119 for fire and ambulance. For English-language assistance, call the Japan Helpline at 0570-000-911, available 24 hours.

### Are there English-speaking hospitals in Nara?

Nara Prefectural General Medical Centre and Nara Medical University Hospital have some English-speaking staff. For smaller clinics, ask at the tourist information centre for recommendations. The Japan Helpline can also assist with medical translation.

### What should I do if I lose my passport in Nara?

File a police report at the nearest koban or police station, then contact your embassy or consulate. The nearest consulates for most Western countries are in Osaka, about one hour from Nara. Emergency travel documents can usually be issued within one to two business days.

### Is Nara safe for tourists?

Nara is extremely safe. Violent crime against tourists is virtually unheard of, and petty crime rates are very low. The main safety considerations are natural disasters (earthquakes, typhoons) and minor health issues, both of which are manageable with basic preparation.

### How do I call an ambulance in Nara?

Dial 119 and say "kyuukyuusha onegaishimasu" (ambulance please). If you cannot speak Japanese, say "English please" — operators have access to interpretation services. Ambulance services in Japan are free of charge.
