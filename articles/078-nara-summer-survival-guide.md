# Summer in Nara: How to Enjoy the Ancient Capital in the Heat

Summer in Nara is hot. There is no delicate way to state this: from late June through September, temperatures regularly exceed 30°C, humidity hovers between 70 and 90 percent, and the combination produces conditions that are genuinely challenging for visitors from temperate European climates. The rainy season (tsuyu, mid-June to mid-July) adds persistent rain to the humidity. August brings the peak heat, with temperatures occasionally reaching 37–38°C.

And yet. Summer in Nara has qualities that the more comfortable seasons cannot replicate. The crowds are thinner. The light is more dramatic. The temples and park have a lush, tropical intensity that the gentle pastels of spring and autumn cannot match. Specific summer experiences — the lotus blooms, the lantern festivals, the evening atmosphere of Naramachi — are available only to those who endure the heat. With proper preparation and realistic scheduling, summer in Nara is not merely bearable but rewarding.

## The Reality of Summer Weather

### June (Rainy Season)

Mid-June marks the beginning of tsuyu. Expect frequent rain, grey skies, and high humidity (though temperatures are moderate: 22–28°C). The rain is not constant — breaks of sunshine alternate with showers. The landscape is at its greenest, moss glows brilliantly, and the deer seek shelter beneath dripping trees.

### July

The rainy season typically breaks in mid-July, replaced by clear, hot weather. Temperatures climb to 30–35°C with intense sunshine. Humidity remains high. This is the beginning of proper summer.

### August

Peak heat. Temperatures of 33–38°C are common. Humidity makes the apparent temperature higher. Late August brings the Obon holiday period, when some Japanese travel domestically, though Nara is less affected than beach destinations.

### September

Temperatures gradually moderate (28–33°C), but summer conditions persist through mid-September. Late September brings cooler mornings and evenings.

## Summer Strategy

### Schedule Around the Heat

The most important summer adjustment: restructure your day around temperature.

**5:30–9:00am**: The golden hours. Cool enough for comfortable walking, temple visits, and park exploration. Dawn in summer is earlier (sunrise around 5:00am in July), and the morning light is beautiful.

**9:00am–4:00pm**: The danger zone. Heat and humidity peak during these hours. Schedule indoor activities, rest at your accommodation, or visit air-conditioned spaces (museums, cafés, shopping).

**4:00–7:00pm**: The second window. As temperatures drop, outdoor activity becomes comfortable again. Evening light in the park is warm and atmospheric.

**After 7:00pm**: Summer evenings are pleasant. Naramachi comes alive with restaurant activity, and the longer daylight extends exploration opportunities.

### Indoor Retreats

**Nara National Museum**: Fully air-conditioned. The Buddhist sculpture gallery rewards extended viewing time that summer's forced indoor hours provide.

**Kofuku-ji National Treasure Museum**: Another excellent air-conditioned option.

**Naramachi cafés**: Perfect midday retreats. An iced coffee or matcha in a cool machiya is a summer pleasure in itself.

**Your accommodation**: A ryokan or hotel room with air conditioning is a summer necessity, not a luxury. Return mid-day, rest, bathe, and re-emerge refreshed.

### Hydration and Protection

- **Water**: Carry at least 500ml and refill frequently. Vending machines (every few hundred metres in the park and city) sell cold drinks for ¥100–¥160.
- **Sun protection**: Hat, sunscreen (SPF 50+), and a hand towel for wiping sweat (a Japanese summer essential).
- **Cooling goods**: Convenience stores sell cooling body sheets, ice packs, and portable fans. These are not novelties — they are Japanese summer survival equipment.
- **Clothing**: Light, loose, breathable fabrics. A spare shirt in your bag (the first will be soaked by mid-morning).

## Summer Highlights

### Lotus Blooms (July–August)

The lotus — a flower of profound Buddhist significance, representing enlightenment emerging from muddy water — blooms in Nara's temple ponds from July through mid-August. Toshodai-ji's lotus garden is the finest, with hundreds of blooms creating a landscape of pink and white above broad green leaves.

Lotus flowers open in early morning and close by midday. Visit before 9:00am for the best viewing.

### Mantoro Lantern Festival (August 14–15)

Kasuga Taisha's Chugen Mantoro is one of Nara's most magical events. The shrine's 3,000 stone and bronze lanterns are lit at dusk, transforming the forest shrine into a landscape of flickering light. The warm summer evening, the ancient forest, and the thousands of small flames create an atmosphere that is genuinely extraordinary.

### Obon Events

Obon (mid-August) — the festival of the dead, when ancestral spirits are believed to return — brings several events to Nara's temples, including Todai-ji's Daibutsu-den Mantoe (August 15), when the Great Buddha is visible through open front windows at night.

### Nara Tokae (August)

An annual light festival that illuminates several locations in central Nara with candles and lanterns. The park, Sarusawa Pond, and other locations create a gentle, romantic atmosphere.

### Shika-no-Tsunokiri (October)

Technically early autumn, but the anticipation builds through late summer. The annual deer antler-cutting ceremony is a traditional event of considerable spectacle.

## Summer Food and Drink

Summer brings specific culinary pleasures:

- **Kakigori**: Shaved ice with flavoured syrup — the quintessential Japanese summer treat. Available at many Naramachi shops and park vendors.
- **Nagashi somen**: Flowing noodles — thin somen noodles flowing down a bamboo chute, caught with chopsticks. A playful summer experience.
- **Cold soba**: Chilled buckwheat noodles with dipping sauce — refreshing and satisfying.
- **Kuzu mochi**: Cold, translucent kuzu-starch sweets — Nara's local speciality at its most refreshing.
- **Beer and cold sake**: Summer evenings in Naramachi call for cold drinks. Local craft beer and chilled sake are excellent options.

## Accommodation in Summer

**Air conditioning**: Non-negotiable. Ensure your accommodation has effective cooling.

**Bath access**: A cool shower or a hot bath after a sweaty day is restorative. Properties with in-room baths provide particular summer comfort.

**Location**: Central Naramachi accommodation like Kanoya minimises walking distances in the heat and allows easy mid-day returns for rest and cool air.

**Yukata**: The lightweight cotton robe provided by ryokan is ideal summer evening wear — cool, comfortable, and appropriate for walks in the neighbourhood.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is summer a bad time to visit Nara?

Not bad — different. Fewer tourists, specific seasonal experiences (lotus, festivals, evening atmosphere), and lower accommodation prices compensate for the heat. But you must adjust your schedule.

### How hot does it get?

July–August daytime temperatures: 30–38°C with 70–90% humidity. Evenings are more comfortable (25–28°C).

### Can I still enjoy the temples in summer?

Yes. Visit in early morning (before 9:00am) or late afternoon (after 4:00pm). The temples are as beautiful as ever — and far less crowded.

### Are the deer affected by summer heat?

The deer are less active during midday heat, seeking shade under trees. Early morning and late afternoon are the best times for deer encounters in summer.
